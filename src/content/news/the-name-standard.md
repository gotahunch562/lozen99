Lozen Advisory today announced the publication of **The Name Standard℠**, a human-attribution standard within the firm's Disclosure-Independent Governance℠ methodology, designed to help corporate boards and executive teams evaluate whether AI-assisted decisions remain traceable to a responsible human or institutional actor.

While standard enterprise roadmaps heavily fund technical data governance, they frequently leave a fundamental board question unresolved: who remains accountable when AI shapes the decision record?

> "The Name Standard℠ fixes this problem by demarcating technology ownership from human accountability," said Akilah E. Kamaria, founder of Lozen Advisory. "A company may have an AI policy, an approved vendor tool, an implementation roadmap, or a generic 'human-in-the-loop' workflow and still lack verifiable evidence for agentic AI-assisted work."

## The Growing Enterprise Governance Problem

Corporate AI adoption has entered a phase where speed outpaces verification, creating board-level accountability blind spots. Corporate governance fails when organizations assign IT ownership without explicitly naming human responsibility.

The Name Standard℠ targets critical operational exposures:

1. **Lack of Verifiable Evidence:** Standard workflows log machine use but fail to capture human review, corrections, escalations, refusals, or sign-offs.
2. **Shadow AI and Agentic AI Scope:** Unapproved AI use and autonomous AI agents make it harder for corporate boards to trace how AI shaped decisions, outputs, or corporate records.
3. **Regulatory and Litigation Pressure:** The AI AGENT Act, The Great American AI Act discussion draft, AI hiring litigation, and the Workday AI bias lawsuit place growing attention on documentation, accountability, and behavioral duties.
