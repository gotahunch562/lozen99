Disclosure-dependent systems create reputational and legal risk because they let an organization believe it has solved a problem it has only documented partially. That belief survives right up until an incident, a lawsuit, an insurance claim, or a director's inquiry forces the organization to produce evidence it doesn't have.

An AI-assisted decision fails in production, and the internal account of what happened turns out to be the same interested party's explanation as the public one — attribution without an actual accountability record behind it. A company can point to an AI governance framework, a usage policy, and an adoption metric, and still be unable to name who had the authority to approve, correct, or stop the specific action that caused the loss. A leadership team can say a human was "in the loop," and be unable to prove that person had the information, time, and standing to exercise real judgment rather than a formality.

That is the moment Lozen Advisory helps clients manage.

The pattern is not limited to AI incidents. Lozen Advisory has documented the same structural failure in workforce systems: an employer may say it offers menopause benefits, yet the women with the most to lose may never use them; a leadership team may say retention is stable, yet its most important people may be carrying strain that will not appear in the data until they leave. Menopause reveals the measurement flaw. AI expands it — into board oversight duties, D&O exposure, and insurance underwriting, where the absence of an accountability record is no longer just a communications problem but a financial and legal one.

Lozen Advisory helps clients respond when that gap becomes visible: reconstructing what evidence actually exists behind an AI-assisted decision, identifying what a board, plaintiff's counsel, or insurer will find when they look for the accountable party, and developing a defensible account of what the organization knew, authorized, and reviewed — before the organization loses control of the narrative.
