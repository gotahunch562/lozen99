The Governance Readiness Briefing answers a single question at a single point in time. The Advisory Engagement is for boards and CFOs who need that question answered on a recurring basis, as AI-assisted decisions continue to accumulate across the organization.

Structured as an ongoing advisory engagement, with virtual or in-person sessions scheduled around board, audit committee, and enterprise decision cycles. Scope and cadence are set according to the number of governance bodies supported, the complexity of the organization's AI exposure, and the level of recurring review required. Lozen Advisory's ongoing engagements are built for boards, board committees, General Counsel, Corporate Secretaries, Chief Risk and Compliance Officers, internal audit leaders, and CFOs who need a standing outside perspective rather than a one-time assessment.

The work applies the Name Standard℠ across recurring, not isolated, AI-assisted decisions: reviewing what authority, review, and evidence existed behind consequential actions since the last engagement; assessing whether the organization's reporting system would satisfy a director's duty under the Caremark line of cases; evaluating proxy and risk-factor disclosure language before it is filed; and tracking how D&O, cyber, and emerging AI insurance products are pricing the organization's own governance evidence, or lack of it.

For CFOs, the engagement addresses whether AI adoption is being governed with the same capital-decision rigor applied to any other material investment — not measured only by an adoption metric that says nothing about who is accountable when something goes wrong.

Lozen Advisory also brings the same accountability standard to workforce risk — benefits strategy, the Power User Trap℠, retention exposure, and leadership continuity — where the same disclosure-dependent measurement failure shows up in a different domain but through identical structural logic.

In stable periods, this work helps boards stay ahead of a fast-moving governance and insurance landscape. In difficult periods, it helps leadership produce a defensible account of what the organization knew, authorized, and reviewed, before the cost of an unresolved accountability gap becomes financial, legal, or reputational.

## What Shapes the Engagement

Buyers are paying for recurring access to an accountability standard, external judgment, and governance continuity across decisions — not for the number of hours spent in a meeting. Scope reflects:

- Organizational and regulatory complexity
- Number of committees or executive functions covered
- Frequency of review
- Volume and materiality of the decisions reviewed
- Whether document, proxy, disclosure, or insurance-language review is included
- Availability between scheduled sessions
- Preparation of board-ready findings or evidence records

## Engagement Format

Sessions are available as a 2-hour or half-day virtual meeting, or a full day of in-person engagement, held on the cadence established above.
